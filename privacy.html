SnakeScale Notes

Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 2, 2026

SnakeScale Notes is a free, open source notes app developed by Chris Gingerich ("the developer"). The app runs entirely in your web browser. The developer operates no servers for this app and does not receive, collect, or store your notes or your personal information. This policy explains what the app itself does with information, on your own device and in your own accounts.

Your notes and files

Your notebooks, notes, and attachments are stored in your own Google Drive, in a folder named "SnakeScale Notes" that the app creates for you. The app requests only the narrow Google Drive permission known as drive.file, which means it can access only the files and folders it creates. It cannot see or open anything else in your Drive. The developer has no access to your Drive, your notes, or your attachments. While the app is open, it also checks Google Drive about every 30 seconds for changes to that folder (a feature you can turn off in Settings) and syncs only when something actually changed.

Your calendar

With your permission, the app also works with your Google Calendar, using the permission known as calendar.events. When a calendar view is open (the Home Calendar widget or the Tasks calendar), the app asks Google for the events in the month on screen and displays them beside your tasks and reminders. At your direction, the app can also create events on your calendars, edit them, and delete them, from its event editor; those changes are made on Google Calendar itself, through Google, when you save or confirm them. Event data is never stored by the app: events are fetched live from Google, held only in the app's memory while it is open, and never stored in your Drive, in the app's files, or on your device. One exception is a link you make between a note and an event: the link is stored as a small marker inside the note itself, and as private app-only data on the event (the ids of the notes linked to it) that only SnakeScale Notes reads and that Google Calendar does not display. Beyond the link ids a note itself carries, the app stores no event data. You can withdraw the permission at any time on your Google account's permissions page; the app then simply shows its calendars without events.

Your email

With your permission, the app also works with your Gmail, using the permission known as gmail.modify. When the Email view is open, the app asks Google for your messages and displays them in the app. At your direction, it can also mark messages read or unread, archive them, apply and remove your labels, move them to the trash (and back), compose and send new messages, reply and forward, and keep drafts in your Gmail account. The permission the app uses cannot permanently delete your mail, and the app does not try. Message data is never stored by the app: messages are fetched live from Google, held only in the app's memory while it is open, and never stored in your Drive, in the app's files, or on your device. One exception exists, and it is yours: a Save to note button in the reading view files the message you are reading as a note in your Drive, and it does so only when you click it. Email bodies are cleaned by the app's own sanitizer before they are shown, and remote images in a message stay blocked until you press that message's Show images control. You can withdraw the permission at any time on your Google account's permissions page; the app then simply shows its Email view as not connected.

With your permission, the app can also read your Google Contacts, through the People API, to autocomplete the To, Cc, and Bcc fields while you compose. Contacts are read live when you use those fields, held only in the app's memory while it is open, and never stored in your Drive, in the app's files, or on your device.

An email link in a note stores only the message's id, plus the sender and subject shown on the link's face; clicking it fetches and opens that message in the Email view.

Microphone and voice notes

The app uses your microphone only when you choose to record a voice note, and only after your browser asks for your permission. The finished recording is stored in your own Google Drive as an attachment, exactly like any other file you attach to a note. Turning speech into text is done by your browser's built in speech recognition service, provided by your browser's maker (for example Microsoft in Edge or Google in Chrome). The app itself sends nothing anywhere for transcription, and the typed words it produces are saved only to your note, in your Drive.

A saved recording can also be transcribed later, from its card's menu. That feature loads a speech model from a public content delivery network the first time you use it. From then on the transcription runs entirely on your device: the app decodes the recording in your browser and the model reads it there. The recording itself is not sent anywhere by the app.

Information stored on your device

So the app opens quickly and works offline, it stores a small amount of information in your browser's local storage on your device: your app settings, a random encryption key created on your device, an encrypted copy of your notes and any changes waiting to sync, and your Google sign-in token kept only in encrypted form. Signing out of the app removes the cached notes, the pending changes, the sign-in token, and the encryption key from that browser. Your Google password is never seen or stored by the app; sign-in is handled by Google.

The sign-in helper

Sign-in runs through a small helper service operated by the developer (a Cloudflare Worker named ssnotes-auth). Google's browser sign-in lasts about an hour and offers the app no way to renew it, so the helper holds the sign-in's Google refresh token on its server side, in its own encrypted key-value store, and hands the app a 30 day session in exchange. The app sends the helper only that session token when it needs a fresh access token; your notes, attachments, and note content never pass through the helper. The helper accepts sign-ins from one Google account only (the developer's). Signing out of the app also ends the session at the helper; the stored refresh token stays there so signing back in is quick, and it can be removed from the helper's store by the developer on request. The permissions the sign in requests are the Drive permission, the calendar permission (calendar.events), and the mail permission (gmail.modify) described in this policy.

Push notifications

Notifications the app raises while it is open are made on your own device, as described below. Separately, the app can register a device for push notifications, so a task or note reminder can reach that device even while the app is closed. Push is off until a device registers for it: registering happens from the Push notifications row in Settings, or on its own when you sign in on a device whose browser already allows the app's notifications, and it never asks for notification permission by itself.

Delivery runs through a second small helper operated by the developer (a Cloudflare Worker named ssnotes-push), which shares the sign-in helper's storage. When a device registers, the browser's push subscription for this app (an endpoint at the push service and a pair of encryption keys the push service uses) is stored there. Each device registers separately, and a device's registration ends when you sign out of the app on it, when you remove it in your browser, or when the push service retires it; signing out of the app on a device removes that device's registration.

To time the pushes, the notification helper borrows the sign-in helper's stored Google refresh token every five minutes and reads two of the app's own files in your Drive: your tasks file (for task titles and due moments) and the notes catalog (for note titles and the reminders set on notes). It reads nothing else, and it writes nothing to your Drive. When a reminder moment arrives, it sends that reminder's notification (the task or note title and its due moment) through the push service of your device's platform, which is Google's push service in Chrome and Android and Apple's push service on iPhone and iPad; those services carry the notification's text to the device, encrypted in transit. The app itself stores no subscription or push data anywhere: a subscription is held only by your browser and by the notification helper.

Services the app contacts

The app connects only to the services listed here, and only as described:

  • Google (accounts.google.com and www.googleapis.com): for sign-in, for reading and writing your notes in your Google Drive, for reading your calendar events when a calendar view is open, and for creating, editing, and deleting events at your direction, and for reading, triaging, composing, drafting, and sending your email when the Email view is open. Google's handling of your information is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.
  • The sign-in helper (ssnotes-auth, a Cloudflare Worker operated by the developer): holds the sign-in refresh token described above and mints fresh access tokens for the app. It receives no note content.
  • The notification helper (ssnotes-push, a Cloudflare Worker operated by the developer): holds the push subscriptions of devices you register and sends reminder notifications to them, as described under Push notifications above. It reads your tasks file and notes catalog from your Drive to time the pushes and receives no other note content.
  • Microlink (api.microlink.io): when you switch a web link inside one of your notes to its Mention or Preview display, the app sends that link's web address, and nothing else, to Microlink to fetch the page's title, description, and images. The contents of your notes are never sent. The information fetched is saved inside your note, in your Drive.
  • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net): when you use the Transcribe feature on an image, the app downloads the text recognition software from the jsDelivr content delivery network. The transcription itself runs entirely on your device; the image being transcribed is not uploaded anywhere. The same network delivers the speech recognition software when you transcribe a saved audio recording, and the model it uses is downloaded on first use; that transcription also runs entirely on your device, and the recording is not uploaded anywhere.
  • Your browser's speech recognition service: when you record a voice note in a browser that offers transcription, the audio is processed by the browser's own built in speech recognition service to turn it into text. SnakeScale Notes does not operate that service and does not receive the audio; the recording itself is saved only to your Google Drive, as an attachment.
  • GitHub Pages: the app's program files are hosted on GitHub Pages. As with any web host, GitHub may record standard request information when your browser loads the app, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

What the app does not do

The app contains no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. No personal information is sold, rented, or shared with third parties for marketing. No human at the developer reads your notes.

Reminder notifications the app raises while it is open are generated entirely on your own device by your browser; nothing about your tasks is sent anywhere to deliver those. The optional push delivery described under Push notifications above is the one exception, and it applies only to devices you register for it.

Google API disclosure

SnakeScale Notes' use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Information accessed through Google Drive is used only to create, read, update, and delete your own notes and attachments, at your direction, in your own Drive account. Information accessed through Google Calendar is used to display your events inside the app while it is open and, at your direction, to create, edit, and delete events on your calendars; it is not stored by the app. Information accessed through Gmail is used only inside the app, to display your messages and, at your direction, to triage, compose, draft, and send your mail; it is not stored by the app.

Your choices and control

You can revoke the app's access to your Google account at any time from the third-party access page of your Google Account settings. Deleting the "SnakeScale Notes" folder in your Google Drive deletes the notes and attachments the app stored there. Clearing the app's site data in your browser removes everything the app stored on that device.

Children

SnakeScale Notes is a general purpose note taking tool and is not directed to children under the age of 13.
